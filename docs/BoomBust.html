Same two pheromones, same stigmergy. Now add stakes: wander too long without a win and an ant dies; deliver enough surplus and the colony spends it on a newborn.

Draw on the world

Drop food to feed a starving colony back to growth, or wall off the easy route and watch the death toll rise until a new trail forms. Add nest places another entrance for the same colony, with no limit on how many — every nest shares the same stockpile and population, just more doors. Move nest grabs whichever nest is nearest and drags it wherever you like. Erase clears walls, and instantly removes food sources and nests you drag over — though it won't remove your last remaining nest. It still leaves a no-entry mark where a source was, so ants already en route learn it's gone. Inspect shows the actual food/home scent numbers under your finger, useful whenever a color on the map is ambiguous.

How this works

Every ant is mortal. Each one carries a stamina clock that resets only on success — finding food, or reaching home. Run out before either happens and it dies (a fading ring marks where). An ant riding an established trail wins constantly and almost never runs the clock down; one exploring blind is gambling with every step. This is a coarse stand-in for real foraging risk, not a literal timer any real ant carries — actual foragers that overstay face rising odds of predation, dehydration, or, for heat-adapted foragers like desert ants (Cataglyphis), fatal overheating. The underlying idea — give up and turn back once a trip has run too long unrewarded — is Charnov's (1976) marginal value theorem and the "giving-up time" it predicts, which has been measured directly in real foraging ants.

Births come from surplus. Every delivery adds to a shared stockpile, and the colony constantly burns a little of it just to exist. Cross the birth-cost threshold and the surplus buys a new worker. There's no population cap — a colony can grow as large as its foraging income supports.

A searcher that's probably lost stops lying. The same stamina clock also governs how strongly a searcher marks its home-scent trail as it wanders: full strength early on, fading toward nothing as it burns through its budget without success. An ant that's likely already lost stops seeding fresh false trails with its most aimless wandering — you can watch this happen directly, since a fading searcher also grows more transparent on screen.

Dead ends actively warn other ants away. When a food source runs dry, a searcher that walks up to it doesn't just wander off — it marks the spot as repellent, and keeps marking a stretch of its way back too. Repeated visits reinforce this "no-entry" signal, the same trick Pharaoh ants use on unrewarding branches (Robinson & Ratnieks) rather than waiting for the old trail to fade on its own. It shows on the map as a dull rust tint, and fades away over time just like ordinary trail scent.

The nest broadcasts too — but it has to earn its range. Close to its entrance, the nest emits a signal of its own, so a carrier reliably finds its way home even if the trail leading there is faint or ambiguous. A brand-new nest starts with only a short-range, weak version of this and grows it slowly with every successful delivery, so a young outpost is genuinely fragile until it's proven itself. Carriers also get a small, constant pull toward the nest's true direction, layered underneath scent-following — real scent always wins when a trail exists; the pull only matters when there's nothing else to follow. Worth flagging as a simplification: this hands the ant a compass rather than modelling how it would actually sense direction.

Wild Terrain generates a maze of walls around the nest and keeps reshaping it — new wall clusters appear every so often, checked to never seal off the nest or an active food source. Food spawns periodically in reachable spots only, checked against a live map of what the colony can actually walk to, so nothing appears sealed behind a wall. Both how often it appears and how much each source holds scale with population — a bigger colony gets fed more often and with bigger drops, so growth doesn't outrun supply.

More nests can be built as additional entrances for the same colony, with no cap on how many — shared stockpile, shared population, just more physical doors. Ants use whichever nest is nearest.

A couple of honest caveats. The founding cohort gets extra stamina on their first trip out, since with no trail yet established their blind odds are much worse than the colony's steady state — without that head start, most runs would die out before any trail could form. If a scenario collapses immediately or grows unchecked, Forage stamina, Birth cost, and Food richness are the first three sliders worth adjusting.
